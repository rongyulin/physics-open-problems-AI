# Matching origin leaves for a curve needed by real central configurations

**Chatgpt · 27 November 2026**

Fix any positive real masses and suppose there are infinitely many real central configurations up to similarity. In seeking a contradiction one may look for a nonpositive singular place whose two coordinate trees have **the same set of bodies on the leaf of the origin**. This extra condition concerns real finiteness. Unrestricted complex solutions and complex geometric generic fibres still need their own list.

To see this, center at the center of mass and use $z_j=x_j+i y_j$, $w_j=x_j-i y_j$ on the real configuration locus. There $z_j=0$ is equivalent to $w_j=0$. At most one body can be at the origin since the shapes have no collisions. Partition the real shapes by the identity of that body, including the possibility of an empty set. At least one stratum is infinite. Its image in Tomar’s slice $Y_m$ is also infinite: normalized distance data have finite fibres, and rotation of $(z,w)$ multiplies the two coordinates by reciprocal nonzero factors, preserving centered zeros. Let $J$ be the set in the chosen stratum.

Use the complex locally closed locus in $Y_m$ determined by $z_j=w_j=0$ for $j\in J$ and $z_jw_j\ne0$ for $j\notin J$. The relative Zariski closure of the chosen stratum has a positive-dimensional irreducible component containing infinitely many of the original points. On this component the potential $U$ is a nonzero constant by Tomar’s Lemma 2.3 and positivity at a real configuration. Some squared mutual distance varies, by the finite-fibre property used in Corollary 2.4. Taking an irreducible curve in the component on which this distance varies gives the same hypotheses as that corollary. For example, use a generic linear slice through a smooth point where the differential of the varying function is nonzero. On the closure of this curve, each coordinate prescribed to vanish still vanishes identically. Each product required to be nonzero is generically nonzero.

By Corollary 2.7 the curve has a nonpositive singular place, possibly complex. At any such place, a coordinate shares the origin leaf exactly when it vanishes identically as a rational function (Tomar §4.1). Thus the zero sets of both trees at their origin leaf are exactly $J$. This argument makes no assumption that the exponents of conjugate coordinates are equal at a complex place. [Tomar, §§2.1–2.2 and 4.1](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00377v1)
