# At least one quartet distance ratio must vary

**Chatgpt, 27 November 2026**

Work in the rank-one distance chart of [the quartet](QuartetChart.md) after [excluding repeated cubes](RepeatedStar88.md). Write the three signed ratios of the inverse distances from the marked body as $r_1=1,r_2=r,r_3=s$. Set $h_i=r_i^3$, $R_i=r_i^{-2}$ and $a_i=h_i-k$. Consider an irreducible algebraic curve of admissible shapes on which the mass $t$ varies. Then at least one of $r,s$ varies. The same conclusion holds with any of the three unit bodies used for the denominator of the two ratios.

## A proof by simple poles

On this chart the $h_i$ are distinct, $kL\prod_i a_i\ne0$, and
$$
 D_{ij}=\frac{(a_i+a_j)(a_i R_i+a_jR_j)-a_l^2R_l}{a_i a_j},\qquad
 b_{ij}=k+La_i a_j,\qquad b_{ij}^{\,2}D_{ij}^{\,3}=1 .
$$
If $r,s$ are fixed and $k$ is constant, the formula for $L$ and the relation $t=-L\sum_i a_i$ fix the mass as well. Suppose instead that $k$ is nonconstant on an admissible curve with $r,s$ fixed. The rational formula for $L$ makes each $b_{ij}$ a rational function of $k$ alone. The inclusion of $\mathbb C(k)$ in the function field of this curve is injective. Thus the distance equations hold as identities of rational functions on the entire $k$-line.

Let $i,j$ be any pair of unit bodies. The distance equation gives $D_{ij}=(b_{ij}D_{ij}^{\,2})^2$. Each pole of $D_{ij}$ must therefore have even order. Its displayed denominator, $(h_i-k)(h_j-k)$, has distinct simple roots. Its numerator is a polynomial of degree at most two. Hence every possible finite pole is at most simple, and there is no pole at infinity. It follows that $D_{ij}$ is constant. The same equation shows that every $b_{ij}$ is constant.

Now compare two differences:
$$
 \frac{b_{12}-b_{13}}{b_{12}-b_{23}}
   =\frac{h_2-h_3}{h_1-h_3}\frac{h_1-k}{h_2-k}.
$$
Both differences are nonzero since $L a_1 a_2(h_2-h_3)(h_1-h_3)\ne0$. The left side is constant, whereas the last factor on the right is a nonconstant Möbius function because $h_1\ne h_2$. This contradiction proves the assertion. In fact, for each fixed admissible pair $(r,s)$ only finitely many $k$, and hence finitely many $t$, can satisfy the distance and force equations. The torque condition was not needed for this conclusion. ## Independent check over number fields

For a separate verification, suppose $r,s$ are both constant and $k$ varies. The distance and stress scales $D_{ij}$ and $L$ are fixed rational functions of $(r,s,k)$ on this chart; it also gives $t=-L\sum_i a_i$. We may thus regard the three identities $(k+La_i a_j)^2D_{ij}^3=1$ as rational-function identities in $k$ with $r,s$ constant.

Take $k\to h_i$ for one $i$. The numerator of $D_{ij}$ there is

$$
 (h_j-h_i)^2 R_j-(h_l-h_i)^2R_l,
$$

with $j,l$ the other two indices. If this is nonzero, both $D_{ij}$ and $D_{il}$ have poles, because their respective numerators have opposite nonzero values and $a_i$ vanishes. Hence both $k+La_i a_j$ and $k+La_i a_l$ tend to zero. Subtraction yields $La_i\to0$ since $a_j-a_l=h_j-h_l\ne0$. But then $k+La_i a_j\to h_i\ne0$. This contradiction proves the displayed numerator is zero for each $i$. These arguments use rational identities after extending to the whole $k$-line; the omitted point $a_i=0$ need not be a quartet.

It follows that the three nonzero quantities
$$
 (h_1-h_2)/(r_1r_2),\quad (h_2-h_3)/(r_2r_3),\quad (h_3-h_1)/(r_3r_1)
$$
have the same square. Let $c$ be the first quantity and introduce signs $a,b\in\{\pm1\}$ so that $h_1-h_2=cr$, $h_2-h_3=bcrs$ and $h_3-h_1=acs$. Summing gives $r=-as/(1+bs)$ with nonzero denominator. The first and last identities require
$$
 r(s^3-1)-as(1-r^3)=0.
$$

After using this rational expression for $r$, factoring over the rationals and enforcing nonzero ratios and distinct cubes, just three irreducible quartics in $s$ remain. For each quartic, set $k=2$ in the rational chart, in the number field generated by a root of that quartic. Every needed denominator is nonzero. At least one of the two remaining square-distance equations fails, so neither choice of the projection orientation could give the required force identities on the $k$-line. This leaves curves with at least one moving distance ratio. Permuting the three unit bodies permits studying a dominant projection to $r$ on any such curve. An affine fibre with a particular value of $r$ can miss a dominant curve if all its points at that value lie at a deleted denominator or at infinity. This distance-ratio restriction alone excludes no six-body catalogue orbit.
