## The equal quartet and the binary torque

All four high coordinates $X_i$ are nonzero and pairwise distinct, and the low coordinates are pairwise distinct. The quartet is noncollinear because its centered projections could otherwise differ only by a nonzero scalar, contradicting $Y_o=0$ and $X_o\ne0$. The quartet forces are independent of $t$. Denote its three star ratios of signed inverse distances by $(1,r,s)$ as in [the zero-projection chart](ZeroProjection88.md). Their cubes must be all equal or pairwise distinct. The relative coordinates, force and distance equations for this quartet are those of [the projective fibre calculation](MixedZeroFibre.md) with **quartet mass parameter one**. The free mass parameter in the binary remains $t$.

In the pairwise-distinct case no such quartet exists, even before imposing the torque. The two integer resultants have degrees 3850 and 3915. The replay reconstructs them using 50 primes and a 2865-bit absolute coefficient bound. Their gcd has degree 3192 and its squarefree part has degree 17. Removing the factors $r$, $r-1$ and $r^2+r+1$ leaves four possible factors for $r$, and the same four for $s$ by symmetry. In each of the 16 product quotient algebras the chart discriminant belongs to the ideal of the three specialized force-distance polynomials. Thus every candidate violates at least one nonvanishing requirement on the chart.

For the equal-cubes case, rescale the relative low coordinates of the other three bodies to $(1,x,-1-x)$. For fixed star roots the relative high coordinates are $d_i=r_i/Y_i$. The weighted high sum $D$ is nonzero since the centered $X_o$ is nonzero. At the equal cubes the subtracted stress coefficient is $k=1$. Zero stress forces the squared relative distance of the first two outer bodies to have cube one; rank-one stress puts the three outer bodies on a line. Clearing the denominators in these alternatives and multiplying gives a nonzero univariate polynomial in $x$ for each of the nine possible ordered pairs of star roots, as checked directly in the replay. Thus only finitely many quartets occur up to the reciprocal projection rescaling. There is also a broader independent finiteness result for fixed positive masses: Hampton and Moeckel establish finiteness of the nine distance equations in the complex algebraic torus. The more elementary fibre argument just given suffices on this zero-projection chart. [Hampton and Moeckel, §6.2, opening paragraph](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~rmoeckel/research/FinitenessRE4BP.pdf)

Fix such a shape, normalize its high coordinates up to scale, and choose square roots of $X_i^{-1}$. The first binary torque requires $S+\mathrm i\rho T=0$, with $S=\sum\epsilon_i/\sqrt{X_i}$, $T=\sum\delta_i/\sqrt{X_i}$, independent signs in each row, and $\rho=t^{3/2}$ or $t^{-3/2}$ depending on the binary order. A common high-coordinate rescaling multiplies both rows by the same factor. Since only finitely many projective shapes and signs occur, persistence for generic $t$ requires both rows to vanish separately. Even one zero row would require
$$
 [(A_o+A_1-A_2-A_3)^2-4A_oA_1-4A_2A_3]^2-64A_oA_1A_2A_3=0,\qquad A_j=1/X_j.
$$
The signs of individual square roots disappear here; the four squared signs in this formula are all positive. Over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ the necessary univariate geometric polynomial, with collision and zero-coordinate factors removed, has gcd one with this torque numerator in each of the nine star-root charts. No such zero row exists. Every remaining shape therefore permits only finitely many $t$.
