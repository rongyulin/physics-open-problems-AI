## Exactly three aligned points

At least two of the three aligned points have mass one. Take their positions to be $(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$, the third $(x,0)$ of mass $m\in\{1,2\}$ and the off-line position $(v,h)$ with $h\ne0$. These coordinates use similarity and translation; the line cannot have isotropic direction because all squared distances on it are nonzero. A common sign reversal of the normalized inverse distances lets the first edge have signed length one. Let $\lambda$ denote the axial force multiplier, with inward acceleration $-\lambda(q_i-q_{\mathrm{cm}})$. For the normalized multiplier only its nonvanishing is needed. The transverse equations at each aligned point give the common inverse cube $k_0=\lambda/5$ on all three edges from the off-line point. In particular, the three squared lengths have the form $S,aS,zS$, with $a^3=z^3=1$. By taking their differences we obtain
$$
 1-2v=(a-1)S,\qquad x(x-1)=[(z-1)-x(a-1)]S .
$$
Since $x\ne0,1$, the displayed denominator is nonzero, and $a=z=1$ is impossible.

Write the signed internal forces of the three aligned points as
$$
 f_0=1+mb/x^2,\quad
 f_1=-1+mc/(1-x)^2,\quad
 f_2=-b/x^2-c/(1-x)^2,\qquad b,c\in\{1,-1\}.
$$
The off-line contribution to the force at axial coordinate $u$ has the common axial expression $m_{\mathrm{off}}k_0(v-u)$. Subtract the axial force equations at two different points. This gives $f_2-f_0=x(f_1-f_0)$ and $f_1-f_0=-(2+m)k_0$. Since $k_0^2 S^3=1$ the second necessary polynomial equation is $(f_1-f_0)^2S^3=(2+m)^2$. For every $m,b,c,a,z$ the two univariate numerators have no common factor outside $x(x-1)[(z-1)-x(a-1)](f_1-f_0)=0$. The last factor must be nonzero by the scale equation.
