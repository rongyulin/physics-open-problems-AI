# Closing the finite boundary of the diagram-88 quartet chart

**Chatgpt, 27 November 2026**

Consider the rank-one distinct-cube [distance chart](QuartetChart.md) with the rational expression for $L$ in [RepeatedStar88.md](RepeatedStar88.md). This note proves that no algebraic family on that chart satisfying the signed torque can have nonconstant $r$. It uses [the upper bound of 4046 for the degree in $s$ of the generic resultant](RatioInfinity88.md), and the exact calculations in [FixedRatio88.md](FixedRatio88.md). Since at least one ratio varies on any family with varying $t$, relabelling the three equal bodies gives the generic-mass exclusion on that chart. The preceding geometric exclusions cover the other quartets.

Write $F,G\in\mathbb Z[r,s,k]$ for the two primitive numerators of the rational force identities, obtained by clearing and cancelling over the integers in the stated chart. Their respective degrees in $(r,s,k)$ are $(82,82,28)$ and $(100,100,34)$. Both specialize at $r=3$ to the numerators in the fixed-ratio proof, up to a constant sign. Their $k$-degrees are preserved there. Put $P=\mathrm{Res}_k(F,G)$, hence $P(3,s)$ is the nonzero resultant of that calculation. The upper bound for $\deg_s P$ is attained at $r=3$, so the leading coefficient in $s$ is a unit at $r=3$. Along an algebraic solution above a place $r=3$, the coordinate $s$ is therefore integral. A complete list of finite limits is needed.

## Local intersection counts

Work over $\overline{\mathbb Q(r)}$. At a simple geometric root of an irreducible boundary polynomial $f(r,s)$, take $f$ as uniformizer. For each chosen center $k_0$, expand $F,G$ in $u=k-k_0$ with coefficient orders $A_j,B_j$ in $f$. For a positive rational $v$, let $d(v)$ be the difference between the largest and smallest indices minimizing $A_j+jv$. For the lower Newton edges with $d(v)>0$, the local intersection number at this center is at least

$$
 \sum_{v>0} d(v)\min_j(B_j+jv).
$$

Indeed the roots of $F$ with $\nu(u)=v$ number $d(v)$ with multiplicity, and each gives the displayed lower bound for $\nu(G)$. Equivalently one may factor off the local monic Weierstrass polynomial of $F$ at the center. All further local intersections over the same $s$ contribute nonnegatively to the order of the homogeneous $k$-resultant, including a possible point at $k=\infty$. Distinct centers can be added. The arithmetic verifier computes each order by exact bivariate polynomial divisions. The four elementary centers are $0,1,r^3,s^3$; when any occurs in the calculation below it is outside the open chart.

The verifier constructs the boundary divisors by factoring the common constant coefficients at these centers, together with two factors from a pair of squared distances. It checks their nonzero leading coefficients at $r=3$, that each specializes square-free, and that their specializations are pairwise coprime. It also checks their complete product with multiplicities against the integer square-free decomposition of $P(3,s)$. Twelve of the eighteen divisors use just the Newton lower bound at elementary centers. The other six treatments are given below. All multiplicities in this note count orders per geometric root in $s$.

**The origin $s=0$.** At $k=0$ the first bound gives 696, from three slopes $1,2,3$ contributing $300,228,168$. At $k=1/2$ the slope $2$ contributes 72. For the slope $2$ at zero, make the further change $k=s^2(1-r^4+w)$ and divide both equations by their minimal powers of $s$. At $w=0$ the new lower-edge count gives a further 24. It counts the excess valuation in $G$ on the branches already counted at slope 2, since $1-r^4$ is nonzero over the function field. This gives the full 792.

**The collision ratios.** At $s=1,\ k=r^3$, the lower-edge count gives 54. After removing its $k=r^3$ factor the gcd of $F(r,1,k),G(r,1,k)$ still has degree 22 in $k$ over $\mathbb Q(r)$. The local intersection over each other common ordinate has length at least its contribution to that gcd, as seen by taking the further quotient by $s-1$. This gives at least 76 in total, all on the boundary $s=1$. For the two old-chart special factors $s=r$ and $s^2+rs+r^2$ we exchange the first two unit bodies. The chart then has $r'=1/r$ and $s'=s/r$. At $r'=1/3$, the reduction modulo 1009 of the universal resultant has order exactly 76 at $s'=1$ and order zero at both primitive cube roots. The two forces retain their $k$-degrees in this computation. Hence $P(r',s')/(s'-1)^{76}$ is a unit at $(1/3,1)$ and $P$ is nonzero at the two other points. A curve on the distinct-cube chart cannot approach any of these points. This change of chart avoids assigning generic multiplicities to the original factors $s-r$ and $s^2+rs+r^2$.

**Simultaneously zero squared distances.** Denote the normalized squared distances $D_{12},D_{13},D_{23}$ by $x,y,z$. Two other irreducible divisors $f$ have bidegrees $(4,4)$ and $(5,5)$ in $(r,s)$ and appear simply in the $k$-resultant of the numerators of $x,y$. On each the quadratic numerators determine a unique common $k$ by subtracting their leading multiples. The denominators in $k$, the three $a_i$, and the other chart units at this intersection are checked explicitly. The simple resultant implies intersection multiplicity one, so $x,y$ are regular local coordinates. At the first divisor $z$ is a unit. At the second it vanishes. All three numerators have nonzero pairwise resultants over $\mathbb Q(r,s)$ and therefore have no common local divisor near this point.

Let $A=a_1a_2$, $B=a_1a_3$, $C=a_2a_3$ and $T=x^3y^3L$. The rational expression for $L$ gives $T=c_1y^3-c_2x^3+c_3x^3y^3$, with $c_1,c_2$ local units and $c_3$ regular. The two primitive numerators here are associates, in the local ring, of

$$
 (k x^3y^3+A T)^2-x^3y^6,\qquad
 z^3(k x^3y^3+C T)^2-x^6y^6 .
$$

Indeed the only possible denominator divisors here are supported on $x,y,z$. Restricting to each possible divisor generically shows the corresponding numerator was not divisible by it. Now blow up with $y=xv$. The leading degree of $F$ is 6; $G$ vanishes to order at least 6 when $z$ is a unit, and at least 9 when $z$ vanishes. At each of the three common simple tangent directions given by $T/x^3=0$ at $x=0$, use $w=T/x^3$ as the second local coordinate. On the strict transform of $F$ the equation reads $(A w+k x^3v^3)^2=x^3v^6$. Each of its two sheets thus gives $\nu_x(w)=3/2$. If $z$ is a unit, $G/x^6$ has order at least 3 on each sheet. If $z$ vanishes, $z/x$ is regular in the blow-up and $G/x^9$ also has order at least 3 on each. The contributions are at least $6\cdot6+3\cdot6=54$ and $6\cdot9+3\cdot6=72$, respectively. These counts concern the forbidden $D_{12}=D_{13}=0$ point.

## Transfer to the varying mass

For each boundary divisor apart from the two handled by relabelling, divide $P$ by the powers accounted for above. These divisions take place over $\mathbb Q[r,s]$ up to constant factors, since the divisors are primitive. The remaining polynomial still vanishes on the projection of any hypothetical admissible family. If the family's projected point over $\overline{\mathbb Q(r)}$ belongs to none of the divisors, this follows from the resultant itself. If it lies on one exactly, its admissible $k$ supplies a local intersection additional to all the forbidden ones used in the division. The two coincident-ratio divisors treated by relabelling are excluded on the open chart in any event. The force polynomials have no common nonconstant factor; in particular there is no projected curve with identically unconstrained $k$ over a moving ratio pair.

At $r=3$ the accounted-for multiplicities match those of the corresponding roots of $P(3,s)$, so no projected family can reach any of those roots. The other two collision groups have already been dealt with in the relabelled fibre. The only remaining possibility is the simple factor of degree 676. The [special-fibre proof](FixedRatio88.md) checks that at least one force polynomial has a unit leading coefficient in $k$ at each of these roots, by its reductions modulo 1009. Thus $k$ is finite, and the unique possible specialization has unit denominators for every expression needed in the torque. The orientation is integral by its monic quadratic. The torque with denominators cleared fails on every such orientation, even when one allows a reduction of a centered coordinate to zero. This eliminates every branch above $r=3$. A nonconstant $r$ on an algebraic curve has a place above $3$ on its projective normalization, yielding the contradiction.
