# From the diagram exclusions to finiteness at a fixed generic mass

**Review note · 27 November 2026**

The intended conclusion concerns real Newtonian central configurations with labelled planar positions, positive Euclidean distances, and masses $(1,1,1,1,1,t)$, taken up to similarity. An algebraic curve without real physical configurations can survive the argument. Genericity is relative to this one-parameter mass family and allows finitely many positive algebraic exceptions.

1. Fix a positive $t$ outside the exceptional sets in the shape and cell arguments. Suppose there are infinitely many of the required real shapes. Tomar’s normalization yields infinitely many points of the algebraic slice $Y_m$. Its map to the 15 squared distances has finite fibres. The physical configurations give a positive signed potential $E$ in this slice. Partitioning by the centered body at the origin, with an extra part for no such body, retains an infinite stratum. The relative Zariski closure of this stratum has a positive-dimensional irreducible component. On that component $U$ is a single nonzero value and at least one squared distance varies. An irreducible curve with varying squared distance can be obtained by slicing. Both $E$ and any other regular function constant on the component still have their physical values on the curve. The condition $z_i=w_i=0$ at designated bodies, and $z_iw_i\ne0$ at the other bodies, also transfers to the generic point of the curve. Reciprocal rotations preserve all these properties.
2. Tomar’s Corollary 2.7 gives a singular place with nonpositive total order of the 15 squared distances. This conclusion and the catalogue containing this place are valid for fixed positive masses; neither invokes the final ambient generic-mass theorem. Under relabelling and exchange of projections the nonpositive total order, the mass marking, and the distance/sign conditions are preserved.
3. The earlier census re-evaluates the explicit core polynomials in $\mathbb Q(t)$, including the re-normalization of a fixed unit mass. The 52,432 one-minimum cells are excluded at all positive masses for such a curve, because their term coefficients are mass products or nonzero multiples of $E$. The 43 cells without a core are handled by separate diagram arguments. Of these, the four-body exceptional sets for diagrams 64 and 66 are closed and omit the all-unit quadruple, so no placement of $t$ is generically contained in them. Diagram 89 is excluded without a mass condition. Diagram 98 requires the separate signed shape and higher-order argument on this mass line. All remaining nonpositive places in the earlier signed list belong to diagrams 88, 93 or 94. The replay from 852 marked placements to 187, or 55 signed orbits, was compared byte-for-byte with the earlier published CSVs.
4. For use of physical estimates at a fixed mass, put $H=\sum_{i\ne j}p_{ij}+z_jw_j/2$ at the marked body $j$. On a dominant irreducible component of the total solution variety the force and signed-distance equations imply $(3/2)dE=H\,dt$. In characteristic zero, derivatives over the mass field therefore annihilate $E$, so it is algebraic over $\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)$. Extending $d/dt$ on the component function field then gives $H=(3/2)E'$, algebraic over the same field. Polynomial equations for these functions persist away from finitely many parameters where all coefficients vanish. Vertical components supply only finitely many further parameter values. At each other fixed $t$, $H$ assumes finitely many values and is constant on any irreducible fibre component, so the curve from Step 1 can be chosen at the value from actual physical configurations. When $E$ equals a candidate algebraic end energy $e(t)$, differentiation can be performed on the incidence variety cut out by $E=e$. Omitting its vertical components and the branch values of $e$ gives $H=3e'/2$ at every remaining intersection. No continuation of real configurations from one mass to another is required.
5. Diagram 93 has a fully circled triple of unit masses in every earlier signed candidate. Its two remaining possibilities put the mark at the regular or partially circled vertex of the other triple. The real energy and the marked derivative exclude both placements. Diagram 94 is excluded for its three possible mark locations directly by signed necessary equations. In diagram 88 an isolated binary containing the mark is incompatible with the physical value of $H$. When the mark is in the quartet the separate projective fibre exclusion allows arbitrary low-coordinate zero patterns. These distinctions give the real finiteness conclusion once the quartet exclusion and remaining local proofs hold. Positive-dimensional signed components with no physically admissible real points do not affect it.
