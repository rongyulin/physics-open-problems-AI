# Specializing any quartet opposite a unit binary

**Chatgpt · 27 November 2026**

This lemma concerns a diagram-88 quartet carrying masses $(2,1,1,1)$ after reduction of the variable mass $t$. It allows all four opposite-projection coordinates to be nonzero at generic parameter. It controls degenerations at the test prime $p=65521$. In particular, checking **all** finite projective quartets over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ at these masses, including the zero force multiplier, suffices for a characteristic-zero statement at generic $t$. [Tomar, Lemmas 6.16–6.17](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00377v1) · [Albouy and Kaloshin, §§3–5](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v176-n1-p10-p.pdf)

**Specialization lemma.** A quartet over $\overline{\mathbb Q(t)}$ satisfying the isolated normalized forces, signed distances and equal-binary torque of diagram 88 gives a projective quartet over an extension of $\mathbb F_p$ with the following properties:

1. Its masses are $(2,1,1,1)$, all six signed inverse distances and all twelve pair differences are nonzero, and one chosen inverse distance is one.
2. Its two centered projections obey the signed quartet equations with a common force multiplier $\lambda$ that can be zero.
3. All four centered high coordinates $X_i$ are nonzero. For a choice of signs and square roots, $\sum_i m_i\gamma_i/\sqrt{X_i}=0$ with each $\gamma_i$ a fourth root of unity. Centered low coordinates can vanish at specialization.

## A valuation over the mass line

Write $t=2+pU$ with transcendental $U$. On $\mathbb Q(U)$ take the Gauss extension of the $p$-adic valuation, which assigns each polynomial in $U$ the minimum valuation of its coefficients. The residue field is $\mathbb F_p(\bar U)$, the mass $t$ has residue 2, and every nonempty subsystem mass is a unit. Extend this valuation to a finite algebraic extension containing all coordinates, inverse distances and square roots in use. Its value group is contained in $\mathbb Q$. All torque phases are units. The algebraic test at the end has coefficients over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, regardless of the extra transcendental element in the residue field. If its affine equations, including inverse variables for the nonzero conditions, have no solution over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, the Nullstellensatz gives the same assertion over any extension.

For $a=z,w$ put $F^a_{ij}=p_{ij}^3(a_i-a_j)$, where $p_{ij}^2(z_i-z_j)(w_i-w_j)=1$. By reciprocal scaling of $z,w$ balance the minimum valuation in the vector of positions and edge forces in each projection. Call the common minimum $v_*$. The balanced scaling requires at most a further quadratic extension and has no effect on the equations or torque. If both minima were positive, each edge difference would have positive valuation in both colors and every edge-force valuation would be negative, an impossibility. If $v_*=0$, each position and force is integral. The two doubled force valuations for a pair with coordinate-difference valuations $A,B$ are $-A-3B,-3A-B$. Since $A,B\ge0$, both being nonnegative gives $A=B=0$ on all six pairs.

If $v_*<0$ renormalize valuations so $v_*=-2$. Circle a body when its position in that color has valuation $-2$ and draw a stroke when the corresponding force term has valuation $-2$. Exactly the possible supports needed here are contained in the five classes of Fig. 8 of Albouy–Kaloshin, independently of characteristic. [BoundaryOrders88.py](BoundaryOrders88.py) checks this by exact rational linear arithmetic from the distance equations, the repeated minima in every coordinate triangle, the mass-weighted force balances of every nonempty subset about each possible anchor, and the differences between individual forces. The translated subset balances and normalization are written in [the boundary audit](BoundaryAudit.md). The test with no identically vanishing low coordinate allows arbitrary finite valuations for all eight projected positions. One can also identify the low ordinate of any chosen body with the origin before forming these constraints, since pair differences allow at most one zero low coordinate. This covers the previously studied zero-projection charts as well.

## Initial coefficients exclude the first four diagrams in characteristic 65521

We spell out which leading equalities are used, since reductions of the final mass conditions simplified using *real* positivity need not apply in finite characteristic. All equations here are evaluated in the algebraic closure of the residue field. [Boundary88.py](Boundary88.py) checks all mass positions and every sign or root of unity. The labels in these cases use the corresponding order of Fig. 8.

- **Disconnected.** Choose the high-projection pair $(1,2)$ and opposing pair $(3,4)$. Their center equations give opposite leading coordinates in each pair in the inverse ratios of their masses. The interpair torque after summing the wedges $m_i q_i\wedge f_i$ over one pair is a four-term signed sum as in (12). Writing $\mu_i^2=m_i$, its necessary equation is $\mu_1^3\mu_3^3\pm \mathrm i\mu_2^3\mu_3^3\pm \mathrm i\mu_1^3\mu_4^3\pm\mu_2^3\mu_4^3=0$. The test prime splits $\sqrt{-1}$ and $\sqrt 2$; changing any choice of square root is included by the independent signs and a common factor. All 32 tests fail.
- **Quadrilateral.** Opposite clusters have equal leading positions within a cluster. Eliminating the two leading positions between the center equations of the two same-color edges gives (14), $m_1m_3=m_2m_4$. No ordering works.
- **Isolated triangle.** Both force components balance internally on its three vertices. Each set of edge-force coefficients is proportional to the cyclically opposite masses, as in (15); their quotient makes the two sets of coordinate differences proportional. The three signed distances therefore give $1/\sqrt{m_1}+\epsilon_2/\sqrt{m_2}+\epsilon_3/\sqrt{m_3}=0$ with $\epsilon_i^4=1$ as in the step before (16). All 64 cases fail.
- **Kite.** Let the double-edge triangle be $1,2,3$, the extra single edge be $14$ in the $w$ coordinate. Every $w$ position is circled, and the three triangle positions have the same leading $w$ coefficient. The remaining position has a different coefficient since the total mass is a unit. Thus $w_{14}$ has valuation $-2$, $z_{14}$ has valuation 2 and $p_{14}$ is a unit. The triangle separations have valuation 1 in each projection. At vertex 4 the $z$ force from body 1 has valuation 2, and the other two terms have valuation $5/2$. Hence $z_4$ and $z_1$ have valuation at least 2. At the triangle scale the origin coincides with the first vertex and is the weighted axial center of 2 and 3. Write $R_{ij}$ for the leading signed distances, the reciprocals of the leading inverse distances. The two internal axial force balances now give $m_1/R_{12}^3=-(m_2+m_3)/R_{23}^3=m_1/R_{13}^3$. Indeed the leading axial positions are proportional to $(0,m_3,-m_2)$. Using $w_{ij}=R_{ij}^2/z_{ij}$ in the closed opposite-projection triangle gives $-1/(m_3R_{12})-1/(m_2R_{13})=1/(m_1R_{23})$, the algebraic relation (19). Set $\eta=R_{13}/R_{12}$; it is a cube root of unity. These two relations require $m_1^2(m_2+m_3)(\eta m_2+m_3)^3=(m_2m_3)^3$. All twelve tests fail.

## The remaining two ways to obtain a projective shape

In the fifth diagram every edge contributes both strong forces, so $-A-3B=-3A-B=2v_*$ for every pair. It follows that $A=B=-v_*/2>0$ and $v(p_{ij})=v_*/2$ independently of $i,j$. Thus either every pair had valuation zero in both projections or every pair collapses at the same positive order. Choose a pair $o,1$ and divide $z,w$ by their respective differences on that pair and all inverse distances by $P=p_{o1}$. This yields integral unit pair differences and unit distance ratios, with the distance equations unchanged. The force multiplier is now $P^{-3}$, with nonzero residue in the first case and zero in the second. Centering is preserved and each centered projected entry is integral since $q_i=M^{-1}\sum_j m_j(q_i-q_j)$ and $M$ is a unit. In the equal-binary torque a common rescaling of all high coordinates can be removed by dividing out the common square-root factor. At most one high coordinate can reduce to zero since their pair differences are units. If one did, only its term would have negative valuation; the three unit terms could not cancel it. Thus all centered high coordinates are units, and the zero torque equality specializes. This proves the lemma.

In the zero-stress case in the resulting fibre, all six normalized inverse cubes agree. A zero multiplier would make them all zero, contradicting the distance equations. In the nonzero case all six normalized squared distances lie in $\mu_3$. For each of the $3^5$ choices left after scaling the first edge to one, form the three-by-three doubled Gram matrix on the relative position vectors from body 0. Its entries are $D_{0i}+D_{0j}-D_{ij}$ with $D_{ii}=0$. Planarity requires determinant zero. [ZeroStress88.py](ZeroStress88.py) evaluates all 243 tuples and obtains nonzero determinants in every case. This elementary zero-stress exclusion also applies in characteristic zero since the prime splits all cube roots of unity.
